# Evaluation methodology and results (Phase 6)

Script: `evaluation.py`. Artifacts: `report/evaluation_results.md` (readable) and
`report/evaluation_results.csv` (per-reference rows). Both are regenerated by one
command:

```powershell
.venv\Scripts\python.exe evaluation.py
```

## 1. Why there are no accuracy / precision / recall numbers

This dataset has **no user ratings, no play histories and no relevance labels**.
There is nothing to compare a recommendation against, so:

* precision, recall, accuracy, F1 and NDCG are **not computed anywhere** in this
  project, and `tests/test_evaluation.py` asserts that `evaluation.py` does not
  even define such functions;
* any claim like "the system is 85% accurate" would be fabricated and is
  forbidden by the brief.

A proper offline evaluation would need at least one of: implicit feedback (plays,
skips), explicit ratings, expert relevance judgements, or a human listening panel.
None exists in this dataset. What follows therefore measures **behaviour**, not
quality, and the report says so wherever numbers appear.

## 2. Measures used

| Measure | Definition | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Intra-list similarity (ILS)** | mean pairwise cosine similarity of the returned rows, computed separately for the numeric block, the genre block, and the weighted combination. Diagonal excluded. | how tightly clustered a recommendation list is. High ILS = homogeneous list; low ILS = adventurous but possibly incoherent |
| **Diversity (genres)** | number of distinct genres across a list | breadth of a single list |
| **Diversity (artists)** | distinct artists ÷ list length | whether one artist dominates a list |
| **Genre consistency** | share of results sharing ≥1 genre with the reference | how "in-genre" the neighbours are |
| **Catalogue coverage** | union of all returned track ids ÷ catalogue size, over all reference tracks | how much of the catalogue the system can reach at all |
| **Duplicate suppression** | full-signature repeats (must be 0) and look-alike title+artist repeats (allowed when audio/duration differ) | whether the approved rule holds and how often variants appear |
| **Self-match exclusion** | reference track appearing in its own list (must be 0) | correctness |
| **Preference influence** | items changed, mean rank shift among surviving items, #1 change share, mean preference match of the adjusted list | how much preferences matter, without claiming they improve quality |

None of these is a relevance metric, and none is combined into a single "score":
weighting them would hide the trade-offs the report is supposed to discuss.

## 3. Reference set (deterministic)

`select_reference_tracks()` builds the set with no randomness:

* for each of **8 genres** (`rock, hip-hop, classical, techno, sleep, jazz,
  acoustic, heavy-metal`) the two most popular tracks (ties by `track_id`);
* plus the **3 most popular tracks with the largest genre sets** (multi-genre).

This yields **17 references** after de-duplication (some tracks qualify for two
genres). Every reference's genre set is recorded in the CSV.

## 4. Protocol

* `k = 10`, content weights 0.90 numeric / 0.10 genre, approved duplicate rule on.
* Baseline: no preferences.
* Preference comparison: three deterministic scenarios — an **aligned** genre (the
  reference's own first genre), a **cross** genre (`classical`), and an **artist**
  preference (`Elton John`).
* All numbers are means over the 17 references unless stated.

## 5. Measured results

### Aggregate

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| mean distinct genres per list | **4.59** |
| mean genre consistency | **0.806** |
| mean artist diversity | **0.900** |
| mean ILS numeric block | **0.9143** |
| mean ILS genre block | **0.5354** |
| mean ILS combined | **0.8764** |
| mean score | 0.9153 |
| full-signature duplicate repeats | **0** |
| look-alike title+artist repeats | **5** (allowed) |
| self-match violations | **0** |
| catalogue coverage | **0.1898%** (170 tracks over 17 lists) |

### Per-reference highlights

| reference | distinct genres | genre consistency | ILS numeric | ILS genre |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| classical | 2 | 1.00 | 0.974 | 0.837 |
| sleep | 1 | 1.00 | 0.950 | 1.000 |
| rock | 6 | 1.00 | 0.923 | 0.288 |
| hip-hop | **8** | **0.20** | 0.944 | 0.092 |
| techno | 8 | **0.30** | 0.889 | 0.089 |

Reading these honestly:

* **Lists are tight.** ILS numeric ≈ 0.91 means the ten results are usually very
  close in audio space; the genre block is much looser (≈0.54) except for
  single-genre references like `sleep` (1.00).
* **Genre consistency depends entirely on the reference.** A pure `hip-hop`
  reference gets only 20% same-genre neighbours at 0.90/0.10 — audio similarity
  pulls in prog-house and heavy-metal tracks. That is expected content-based
  behaviour, not a defect, but it means the catalogue's genre labels do not
  describe the recommended sets.
* **Coverage is tiny by nature.** 17 short lists can only touch 0.19% of 89,583
  tracks; content-based systems are inherently local, and this number should be
  read as "reach", not "usefulness".
* **Correctness holds.** Zero self-matches and zero full-signature duplicates
  across all 17 lists.

### Preference influence

| scenario | mean items changed (of 10) | mean rank shift | top-1 changed | mean preference match |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| aligned genre | **8.59** | 4.65 | **94%** | 0.876 |
| cross genre | 3.59 | 1.79 | 35% | 0.400 |
| artist | 3.82 | 2.89 | 65% | 0.394 |

Overall: mean changed items 5.33, mean rank shift 2.78, top-1 changed in 64.7% of
cases, mean preference match of the adjusted list 0.557.

Reading these:

* An **aligned** preference reshapes the list almost completely (94% of #1
  results change) — expected, since the preference reinforces a genre the
  reference already belongs to.
* A **cross** preference (classical) is close to an unimplemented preference for
  most references: it moves ~3.6 items and matches only 40% of the list, because
  few candidates are classical.
* The **artist** scenario moves fewer items than aligned genre but still changes
  the top result 65% of the time; artist matches are rarer than genre matches.

## 6. Interpretation and trade-offs

1. **High ILS is not a success metric.** Tight lists are predictable and
   defensible for a similarity-based system; they also mean low discovery.
   Reporting ILS without that caveat would misrepresent it.
2. **Genre consistency is a property of the reference, not of the system.** It
   ranges from 0.20 to 1.00 across references at identical weights.
3. **No number here says "these recommendations are good".** Only structural
   correctness and measured behaviour are established.
4. **Preference effects are bounded by design** (0.20 of the score) and measured
   by movement, not by usefulness.

## 7. Limitations

* No relevance labels → no quality metric, no comparison against a baseline
  recommender, no human judgement recorded.
* Only 17 references; genre coverage is broad but each genre contributes 1–2
  references, so per-genre conclusions are indicative, not general.
* `k = 10` only; results at 5 or 20 were not evaluated.
* Coverage is computed over these lists only, not the full recommendation space.
* Look-alike repeats (5) are permitted by the approved rule and counted, not
  corrected.
* ILS/coverage are sensitive to catalogue size and to the 114-genre one-hot
  representation.
* Human musical judgement (task 6.2) is intentionally left open: the sampled
  top-5 tables are in `report/evaluation_results.md`, but only the student can
  judge whether they are musically sensible.

## 8. Reproducibility

* `evaluation.py` is deterministic: reference selection uses popularity and
  `track_id` only; no sampling, no seeds.
* Re-running it regenerates both artifacts byte-for-byte on the same data.
* `tests/test_evaluation.py` (15 tests) covers the pure metrics, the structural
  invariants on the real data, the preference comparison, and the guard that no
  precision/recall-style function exists.
